All mistakes · Section 4 · A rubric that separates two runs

A run that does not fail enough of the rubric

fails the task
Hard Client Requirements for Every Task
1

What the issue is

The task is built, the rubric is written, and the criteria Model A failed add up to less than half of the rubric's weight, both blocks counted. From inside the task nothing looks wrong: the criteria are sound and the run does fail some. What gives it away is the arithmetic.

2

Why it matters, and what the project expects

Every task has to be hard enough that the Model A trajectory clearly fails at least 50% of the rubric score. That is a hard client requirement, not a target. A run that sails through most of the set is not separating a strong model from a weak one, and the data is worth less.

Attempter GuidelinesHard Client Requirements for Every Task
Model A has to fail: Enough difficulty that the Model A trajectory clearly fails at least 50% of the final rubric score, with failures that materially affect task completion and the user experience. If the desired outcome is reached too easily, increase the complexity.

Attempter Guidelines8.5 Rating Against both Models and Justifications
Rate each subjective rubric as Present or Not Present for Model A.

Model A is rated on the subjective block too, so the 50% is taken over both blocks.

Attempter Guidelines1.2 Building the Idea
The idea is the foundation of your entire task; if it's rushed or underdeveloped, the agent will complete the task too easily, and you won't be able to meet the core requirement that the agent must fail at least 50% of the final rubric score.

The fix is always a harder scenario, never heavier weights on the same rows.

3

How to recognise it in your own task

List every criterion Model A failed, in both blocks: each positive rated Not Present and each negative rated Present. Add up their weights, ignoring the minus sign, and divide by the total weight of every criterion in the task, counted the same way. Your failure justifications already name every one of them, so this is a number you can work out before you submit.

5

What to do instead

Under 50% means the scenario is too easy. The fix is a harder scenario, putting the sources in disagreement, never heavier weights on the same rows, and never padding the set.

The issue, in this task
The setup
The page has to reconcile a May quote against the current one, price the financing and lay out two purchase paths.
Where the issue shows up here
Thirteen of the forty nine criteria carry a failure justification, all thirteen in the objective block. Their weights add up to 43, against a total weight of 123 across both blocks.
Why that is wrong
Model A failed 35.0% of the rubric weight, well under the 50% the client requires. The failed rows already carry more weight on average than the passed ones, so heavier weights are not the answer: Model A got most of the task right.
What it should have said
A harder scenario, settled before the run, where more of the page rests on sources that disagree and have to be reconciled. Not heavier weights on the thirteen rows it failed, and not extra rows written for it to fail.
The rules it breaks

Sunniva Haugland owns Storvik Bakeri at 808 Commercial Avenue in Anacortes. She has been trying to decide whether to buy a Doyon three‑deck oven since April.
