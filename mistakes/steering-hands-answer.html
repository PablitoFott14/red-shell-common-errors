All mistakes · Section 2 · What the conversation may not say

A steering turn that hands over the answer

fails the task
Multi-Turn - Simulator Answer Leak
1

What the issue is

A user turn goes past saying something is wrong and supplies what the agent was meant to work out from the inputs: a value, a count, a position or the conclusion itself. It usually appears after two or three softer turns failed to move the run. Naming the exact file to open stops one step short: it hands over no value, so it costs points rather than the task, but it is the same habit.

2

Why it matters, and what the project expects

Every turn you type as the user is graded, steering turns included, and a leak fails the task however late it comes. A criterion satisfied by following an instruction no longer separates a strong model from a weak one. It is also how wrong answers get into a rubric: a turn that dictates can dictate incorrectly, and the criteria then get written to match it. The same applies to a turn that settles a question one of your negatives exists to catch: nothing states a value, and the negative can still never fire.

QC specMulti-Turn - Simulator Answer Leak
The no-answer-leak rule extends to everything the CB types as the simulated user, on every turn. The user may say THAT something is wrong; they may not say WHAT the right answer is, WHERE exactly to find it, or HOW MANY of anything there are — point at the area, never at the value. This is the most frequently broken rule in the multi-turn format because it feels like helping. Read every user turn, not just the opening prompt.

Every turn you type counts, including the ones you sent only to unstick the golden. A value, count, position or conclusion fails the task; naming the exact file to open is over steering and costs points.

Attempter Guidelines1.2.3 Create the Initial Prompt and the Follow-ups
Never flag the miss. If the agent omitted a total, misread a date, or ignored a source, do not tell it what went wrong or where to look. Pointing directly at the failure compromises the 50% failure threshold every task has to meet.

That is the Leg A rule: a follow up never says what went wrong. In Leg B a hint may say what is still missing, never the answer.

Attempter Guidelines7.2 Steering the Run & Hinting
A hint explains what is still missing from the user's point of view. It should never tell the model what to write or how to solve it. The test is simple: if the model can copy your hint into the artifact or use it directly as the reasoning needed to reach the milestone, you have given away the answer.

The copy test is the one to run on every steering turn before you send it.

3

How to recognise it in your own task

Read each of your turns and ask what is left for the agent to work out. Then read your negative criteria against the same turns and check each one is still reachable.

4

Real tasks where this happened

Two real tasks in this project are carrying it. They are evidence that it is real and something to practise spotting it on, not a second version of the rule. Open one and it expands here, with the point on the left and the task's own fields on the right. Escape closes it.

5

What to do instead

Write every steering turn in the voice of the person in the scenario. Say something looks wrong and point at the area, never at the file, the value or the conclusion. If a turn would rule out the trap one of your negatives exists to catch, rewrite the turn, not the criterion. If the model still misses, send another hint that still points only at the area; if the scenario itself turns out infeasible, redefine it rather than sharpen the hint.
