A steering turn that hands over the answer

fails the task
Multi-Turn - Simulator Answer Leak

What the project asks for gl §3 · spec · Multi-Turn - Simulator Answer Leak

You are the user, not a second author. A follow up may say something looks wrong and point at the area; it may not supply the file, the value, the structure or the conclusion. Pointing directly at the failure “compromises the 50% failure threshold every task has to meet”, and any leak of a graded value fails the dimension however late in the conversation it happens.

What the mistake is

A user turn goes past saying something is wrong and supplies what the agent was meant to derive: the exact file to open, the value, the structure of the answer, or the conclusion. It usually appears after two or three softer turns failed to move the run.

How to recognise it in your own task

Read each of your turns and ask what is left for the agent to work out. Then read your negative criteria against the same turns and check each one is still reachable.

What to do instead

Write every steering turn in the voice of the person in the scenario. Say something looks wrong and point at the area — never at the file, the value, the structure or the conclusion. If the model still cannot get there, the scenario needs fixing, not the hint.

Why it costs you

The steering turns are not graded, but what they produce is. A criterion satisfied by following an instruction no longer separates a strong model from a weak one. It is also how wrong answers get into a rubric: a turn that dictates can dictate incorrectly, and the criteria then get written to match it. The same applies to a turn that settles a question one of your negatives exists to catch — nothing states a value, and the negative can still never fire.
