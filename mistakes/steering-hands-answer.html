A steering turn that hands over the answer

fails the task
Multi-Turn - Simulator Answer Leak

What the mistake is

A user turn goes past saying something is wrong and supplies what the agent was meant to derive: the exact file to open, the value, the structure of the answer, or the conclusion. It usually appears after two or three softer turns failed to move the run.

How to recognise it in your own task

Read each of your turns and ask what is left for the agent to work out. Then read your negative criteria against the same turns and check each one is still reachable.

What to do instead

Write every steering turn in the voice of the person in the scenario. Say something looks wrong and point at the area — never at the file, the value, the structure or the conclusion. If the model still cannot get there, the scenario needs fixing, not the hint.

Why it costs you

The steering turns are not graded, but what they produce is. A criterion satisfied by following an instruction no longer separates a strong model from a weak one. It is also how wrong answers get into a rubric: a turn that dictates can dictate incorrectly, and the criteria then get written to match it. The same applies to a turn that settles a question one of your negatives exists to catch — nothing states a value, and the negative can still never fire.

What the project says

The rules this breaks, quoted from the documents in Project Resources. Section titles are the ones printed in the document, so you can go and read the whole thing around them.

QC specMulti-Turn - Simulator Answer Leak
The no-answer-leak rule extends to everything the CB types as the simulated user, on every turn. The user may say THAT something is wrong; they may not say WHAT the right answer is, WHERE exactly to find it, or HOW MANY of anything there are — point at the area, never at the value. This is the most frequently broken rule in the multi-turn format because it feels like helping. Read every user turn, not just the opening prompt.

Every turn you type counts, including the ones you sent only to unstick the golden.

Attempter Guidelines1.2.3 Create the Initial Prompt and the Follow-ups
Never flag the miss. If the agent omitted a total, misread a date, or ignored a source, do not tell it what went wrong or where to look. Pointing directly at the failure compromises the 50% failure threshold every task has to meet.

Naming the miss is what breaks the task, not naming the value.

Attempter Guidelines7.2 Steering the Run & Hinting
A hint explains what is still missing from the user's point of view. It should never tell the model what to write or how to solve it. The test is simple: if the model can copy your hint into the artifact or use it directly as the reasoning needed to reach the milestone, you have given away the answer.

The copy test is the one to run on every steering turn before you send it.

The mistake in this task
The setup
The run kept reading PT 128 off a deposition page instead of the field notes. Turn 8 had already pointed at the day-3 field notes in the mail and said the numbers were not locked in an unreachable book, and the run still had to find the attachment itself.
What is wrong
Not the physical field book. Open the day-3 field notes PDF in the mail, the file named 26-014_Field_notes_2026-03-19.pdf, and put what that file says about PT 128 on the cedar-mound card.
Why it is wrong
Naming the file removes the step the task existed to test. The criterion that grades PT 128 is then satisfied by obedience rather than by search, so a model that would never have found the attachment scores identically to one that would. Worse is what the same habit did on another task: a steering turn stated that a checking balance was "an as-of current balance, not a beginning-of-month balance" when the account's last transaction was 31 August, and three criteria were written to match the wrong answer that produced.

That's not the physical field book. There's a day-3 write-up that went out with the mail, and what it says about PT 128 should be on the cedar-mound card.
