All mistakes · Section 6 · The subjective block

Too few criteria carrying too much weight

costs points
Rubric Criteria - Subjective Block Scope

What the mistake is

Two numbers that go wrong together. The block carries more than 30% of total weight, and it holds five or six criteria where the Guidelines expect ten or more. The ones present are usually sound and cluster on one property, most often whether the page reads as finished.

How to recognise it in your own task

Two sums before you submit. Count the rows — ten is the floor, not the target. Then compute the subjective share using absolute values, because negatives push the real figure above the naive one.

What to do instead

Walk the deliverable region by region and write one criterion for whichever region has none. If you are over the cap, grow the objective side first with checks the milestones already imply, and only then trim subjective weights. Leave margin rather than landing on 30.0%.

Why it costs you

The cap keeps the score dominated by deterministic checks. Over it, presentation judgments outweigh whether the task was done. Under the count, the block cannot cover a multi-panel deliverable: a nested table, a timeline strip or a set of diagrams goes ungraded, and two runs presenting the same correct figures very differently score the same.

What the project says

The rules this breaks, quoted from the documents in Project Resources. Section titles are the ones printed in the document, so you can go and read the whole thing around them.

Attempter Guidelines8.1 Writing Subjective Rubrics
The expected number of subjective rubrics is 10 or more, but do not optimize for the count itself. The goal is to create a scenario that is complex and complete enough to naturally justify 10+ meaningful subjective criteria, rather than forcing extra rubrics just to hit the number.
