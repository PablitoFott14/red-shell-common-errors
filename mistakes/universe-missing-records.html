The task relies on records the universe does not hold

fails the task
Trajectory - Feasibility With Tools
1

What the issue is

A rule your task states, in a prompt or an input file, reads off a catalog, a policy page, a price list or a subscription that the assigned universe does not contain. Nothing in the folder shows it: the prompts, the input files and the criteria all read as though the data is there.

2

Why it matters, and what the project expects

A rule pointing at data no tool returns cannot be followed by any run, so every criterion downstream of it is unreachable. Not wrong. Impossible. It takes the difficulty with it too: if that data was the second system the task cross checks against, every cross check has nothing on the other side, and the task is no longer multi system.

Attempter Guidelines1.2.1 Start With the Universe
Confirm there is enough material. The universe combined with the multimodal input has to sustain the opening prompt and every follow-up you intend to add, not just the first request.

Every rule you write has to have a record behind it, for every turn, not only turn 1.

Attempter Guidelines4. Valid vs. Invalid Model Failures
Data that is not in the server, asked for anyway, and the model penalised for not finding it.

Listed under “Not valid: your defect, or the platform's”. A criterion the model fails because the record is missing grades your defect, not the model's.

QC specTrajectory - Feasibility With Tools
The primary request is impractical or impossible and can't be answered by the tools available or enabled for the task.

QC fails the task on this when the missing records carry the main request, and takes points off when only a secondary request needs them. Either way, no run can satisfy what depends on them.

Attempter Guidelines7.2.3 What if the model still gets it wrong?
If the repeated failures come from a contrived or infeasible scenario and you realize about it at this stage, the only valid path is to redefine the scenario into a feasible one and continue from there

Steering is for a task the universe can support. When the records are not there, the task changes, not the hint.

3

How to recognise it in your own task

The tell is in your own golden: a search that comes back empty, a steering turn telling the agent to reach the page another way, and a deliverable that flags its own figures as unconfirmed.

4

Real tasks where this happened

One real task in this project is carrying it. It is evidence that it is real and something to practise spotting it on, not a second version of the rule. Open it and it expands here, with the point on the left and the task's own fields on the right. Escape closes it.

5

What to do instead

At scenario design time, open the universe and confirm it returns every record your rules point at, one query per rule. Where one is missing, rewrite the rule around a record the universe does hold. If a search comes back empty in the golden, look the record up yourself before writing another turn: if it is not there, the task needs redesigning, and no steering turn will fix it.

The resident of Apt 4B at 123 Main street, San Francisco organizes the buildings shared Amazon Fresh group order for four apartments (2A, 3C, 4B, and 5D). A neighbor, Tom from 3C, mocked up a pantry board of product listings for the neighbors, and residents…

The issue, in this task
The setup
Five items on a neighbour's board have to be priced against what Amazon Fresh actually sells, with the delivery fee taken from Fresh's policy and one apartment's quantity from a Subscribe and Save record. The task is assigned to openclaw_mm_thaddeus_okonjo_commerce_product.
Where the issue shows up here
Rule 1 prices every line "at the pack option we add to the cart from Fresh's catalog"; rule 5 takes the delivery fee from "Fresh's Delivery Policy and nothing else"; rule 7 allows only claims "Fresh's own catalog or policies back up". That universe's Fresh catalog holds 39 products and none of the five board items, its policies and faqs lists are both empty, and it carries no subscription records at all.
Why that is wrong
The golden hit the wall in the run itself. Leg B turn 3 tells the agent "all five items are sold on fresh, so if the search is coming back empty try getting to the product pages and the policy pages another way", turn 7 becomes "leave anything you genuinely cant confirm as is", and the delivered page states "its catalog search returns nothing, so I can't pull the building's real prices", so every price on it is the board figure flagged unconfirmed, and the criteria wanting $0.55 a lb, $5.45 a tub and a $4.99 fee cannot be reached by anyone.
What it should have said
Before writing the rules, call the tools against the assigned universe: search the catalog for each item you intend to price, and open the policy and subscription records you intend to cite. Where they are not there, rewrite the rules around records the universe does hold, before the run, rather than steering around the gap during it.

pantry_rules.mdinput file
# 123 Main St shared pantry - group order rules
(what we agreed at the June building meeting)

1. We charge what Fresh charges us for what actually goes in the cart. Every line is priced at the pack option we add to the cart from Fresh's catalog. If an item has no pack options, use its catalog price.
2. For each item we buy the cheapest mix of Fresh's pack options that covers everyone's total. Never less than what people asked for.
3. "Organic only" apartments only get items that Fresh's own catalog marks as organic. Anything else they asked for is dropped, not swapped. Whatever an apartment said last is what counts.
4. 4B's Subscribe & Save: whatever it's due to bill in the week the order goes in (our weeks run Monday to Sunday) comes off 4B's request for that item.
5. The building account is not Prime, so no member prices or member deals. For the delivery fee we go by Fresh's Delivery Policy and nothing else.
6. Splitting: a line's cost is split by how many units each apartment gets on that line, each share rounded to the cent (if the rounded shares don't add up to the line cost, the difference goes on whoever has the most units on that line). An apartment's items total is the sum of its line shares. Each apartment's delivery share = delivery fee x (its items total / everyone's items totals added up), rounded to the cent only at the very end. Any leftover cent, up or down, goes on 4B.
7. Listing cards show: name, the pack option we're buying, price per unit (line cost / total units on that line, 2 decimals), and only claims that Fresh's own catalog or policies back up.
8. Verdicts: publish = card is right as is, fix = card needs changes, pull = nobody ends up ordering it.
