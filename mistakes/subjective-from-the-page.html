All mistakes · Section 6 · The subjective block

Subjective criteria written from the finished page

costs points
Rubric Criteria - Overfitting

What the project asks for gl §8.1 · appendix

Subjective criteria name “ONE observable property of the RENDERED artifact”, graded against the golden side by side — layout, legibility, hierarchy, structure, consistency. A property, not the wording one build happened to use.

What the mistake is

A subjective criterion names the exact heading, button label, row label or column title your golden page happens to use, and requires it. The property underneath is usually real and gradeable. The criterion asks for the wording instead of the property, and none of the quoted strings appears in any prompt — it is your own copy, read back off your own build.

How to recognise it in your own task

Cover the page and read the criterion again. If it only makes sense with your build in front of you, it is naming your text rather than a property.

What to do instead

Write each one from the property down. A label the user fixed in a prompt can be required; a label you chose cannot.

Why it costs you

Subjective rubrics grade how the artifact reads: layout, hierarchy, spacing, consistency. A criterion naming one build's text rejects a subset of valid implementations, and a second correct run presenting the same findings under its own wording fails almost the whole block. Where most of the block is written this way, the count alone carries the task past the failing threshold, so rewriting three or four is not enough.

Every prompt the task ships
Turn 1
It's the 28th of August and I'm trying to close out something that's been sitting in the back of my head. I drove my own car to Everett twice this year for work, once in May and once in August, and I filed a mileage claim for each trip. Marguerite approved both of them and HR wrote back saying they were acknowledged and complete and that the money comes through payroll, no separate deposit, nothing else needed from me. Which is fine, except my paycheck is the same number every single time and I honestly can't tell whether either one of those ever actually landed. I put some things in inputs for you, the expense policy and a couple of my pay statements, plus something the bank sent me. My mail and my accounts should have the rest. Can you work out, for each of the two claims separately, whether the money reached me or not? I don't want them added together into one number, I want to know which one is which. And if one of them didn't come through, tell me whether I can still do anything about it, because I have a feeling the policy puts a clock on that. Put it together as reimbursement_status.html. I want to be able to open it and read it properly before I send anything to Payroll.
Turn 2
I found another pay statement and attached it as IMG_4471.jpg. Please check it against the policy, the claim emails, and the account records, then update the same reimbursement_status.html with whatever it changes. Keep the two claims separate and preserve everything that remains supported.
Turn 3
Before I send this, for any claim you determine is overdue and unpaid, put the discrepancy deadline on the page as an exact date and say whether it had already passed as of the 28th. Keep using reimbursement_status.html, keep the two claims separate, and preserve everything that remains supported.
What is wrong
"In reimbursement_status.html, two claim cards sit in one row, headed 'Claim 1 - May trip' on the left and 'Claim 2 - August trip' on the right." and "...under 'The determination (as of 28 August 2026)', a four-column table has the header row Claim, Reached your account?, Due by 28 Aug?, and Verdict."
Why it is wrong
Nine of the ten subjective criteria are written this way, which is 39% of all twenty-three criteria in the task and over every severity threshold. A page that answers both claims correctly, separates receipt status from due status, and heads its cards by month rather than by 'Claim 1' loses nine of ten subjective points for wording the author never asked for in any prompt.
What it should have said
"In reimbursement_status.html, the two claims are presented as two cards side by side rather than one after the other, each headed with the month of its trip." and "In reimbursement_status.html, the determination table gives each claim its own row, with receipt status and due status in separate columns rather than combined into one verdict."
