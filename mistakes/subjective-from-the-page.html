All mistakes · Section 6 · The subjective block

Subjective criteria that require your golden's wording

costs points
Rubric Criteria - Overall Rubric Quality
1

What the issue is

A subjective criterion names the exact heading, button label, row label or column title your golden page happens to use, and requires it. The property underneath is usually real and gradeable. The criterion asks for the wording instead of the property, and none of the quoted strings appears in any prompt: it is your own copy, read back off your own build.

2

Why it matters, and what the project expects

Subjective rubrics grade how the artifact reads: layout, hierarchy, spacing, consistency. A criterion naming one build's text rejects a subset of valid implementations, and a second correct run presenting the same results under its own wording fails almost the whole block. Overfitting counts toward the share of criteria built wrong, and the task fails once that share passes 15% of all its criteria. Where most of the block is written this way, rewriting three or four is not enough.

QC spec appendixMulti-Turn Authoring Standards · Writing Subjective Rubrics
Name ONE observable property of the RENDERED artifact. The judge sees the render, not the intent behind it. TEST: if two careful reviewers looking at the same render would disagree on PASS/FAIL, the criterion is not specific enough. Name the property.

A property, not the wording one build happened to use.

Attempter Guidelines8.1 Writing Subjective Rubrics
Focus on the render side never asked in the prompt: layout, visual hierarchy, spacing, readability, styling, structure, tone/register, consistency, and overall polish. If anything related to the render is asked in the prompt, it requires an objective rubric ONLY.

A label the user fixed in a prompt is graded in the objective block only. The wording of a label you chose is not graded anywhere.

QC spec appendixRubric Quality Definitions · Overfitting and Underfitting
Overfitting: Criteria that are overly specific, inflexible or too rigid - they incorrectly reject a subset of valid implementations. Criteria must be flexible enough to accept different valid implementations.

A second correct run presenting the same results under its own wording fails every criterion written this way.

3

How to recognise it in your own task

Search your prompts for every heading, label or column title a subjective criterion quotes. If no prompt contains it, you chose it, and the criterion is grading your wording. If a prompt does, it belongs in the objective block.

4

Real tasks where this happened

One real task in this project is carrying it. It is evidence that it is real and something to practise spotting it on, not a second version of the rule. Open it and it expands here, with the point on the left and the task's own fields on the right. Escape closes it.

5

What to do instead

Write each one from the property down: name the element by what it is, such as the summary table or each card, and grade how it is presented rather than what it is called. A label the user fixed in a prompt belongs in the objective block.

Noor Al-Bazzaz is a lead technical illustrator at Chinook Technical Publications, a small technical documentation firm in Yakima, Washington. In the past few months she drove her own car to two client visits in Everett and filed a mileage claim for each trip…

The issue, in this task
The setup
Three turns asking for a single page resolving two expense claims separately, saying for each whether the money reached the user's account and whether the policy clock still allows her to raise it.
Where the issue shows up here
Criteria 1 and 4 require a card heading and a table header row copied off the golden page: "In reimbursement_status.html, two claim cards sit in one row, headed 'Claim 1 - May trip' on the left and 'Claim 2 - August trip' on the right." and "...under 'The determination (as of 28 August 2026)', a four-column table has the header row Claim, Reached your account?, Due by 28 Aug?, and Verdict."
Why that is wrong
Nine of the ten subjective criteria are written this way, and no prompt asks for any of the words they quote. A page that answers both claims correctly, separates receipt status from due status, and heads its cards by month rather than by 'Claim 1' can fail all nine for wording nobody asked for.
What it should have said
"In reimbursement_status.html, the two claims are presented as two cards side by side rather than one after the other, each under its own heading." and "In reimbursement_status.html, the determination table gives each claim its own row, with receipt status and due status in separate columns rather than combined into one verdict."

Turn 1
It's the 28th of August and I'm trying to close out something that's been sitting in the back of my head. I drove my own car to Everett twice this year for work, once in May and once in August, and I filed a mileage claim for each trip. Marguerite approved both of them and HR wrote back saying they were acknowledged and complete and that the money comes through payroll, no separate deposit, nothing else needed from me. Which is fine, except my paycheck is the same number every single time and I honestly can't tell whether either one of those ever actually landed. I put some things in inputs for you, the expense policy and a couple of my pay statements, plus something the bank sent me. My mail and my accounts should have the rest. Can you work out, for each of the two claims separately, whether the money reached me or not? I don't want them added together into one number, I want to know which one is which. And if one of them didn't come through, tell me whether I can still do anything about it, because I have a feeling the policy puts a clock on that. Put it together as reimbursement_status.html. I want to be able to open it and read it properly before I send anything to Payroll.
