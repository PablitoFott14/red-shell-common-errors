2 · The allowance — what it is & can it buy the oven

NO — it cannot pay for an oven bought before the new term

What Cap Sante actually wrote (16 & 18 June; on 26 Aug Delphine wrote, "Nothing has been withdrawn and nothing has been amended"):

  • It is a reimbursement, not a rent credit — and "it cannot be taken as one."
  • Cap to $18,000, against paid invoices for work at the premises.
  • Scope must be approved in writing before work begins.
  • Invoices submitted within 12 months of commencement — and commencement is 1 July 2030.
  • It exists only if you sign the 5-yr renewal (2030–2035, $24.75/sq ft NNN, 3% compounding). Deadline to accept: 15 Jan 2027.
Why it doesn't touch a 2027 oven: the reimbursement window opens at the 2030 commencement, so a 2027 purchase falls years outside it and before any pre-approval could exist. Odalys, 26 Aug: "The 18,000.00 does not exist unless you exercise the option… money attached to a signature on a five year term beginning 1 July 2030, not money attached to an oven."

3 · Can the building take the oven as quoted?

NO — not without the three-phase upgrade

From Peder's note left after the panel, 24 July:

  • Building main: 200A, 240/120V — single-phase (1ph).
  • Everything running ≈ 148A (of 200A).
  • Walk-in + mixer still share one leg — "next job."
  • Doyon needs 45A at 208V, three-phase (3ph).
On amps alone it would have just fitted: 148A running + 45A = 193A of the 200A main, ~7A to spare. But that is academic — a 240/120V single-phase service cannot supply 208V three-phase at all, so the limit is the phase, not the amperage. This is exactly the wall you hit in May, and the reason Fidalgo's $15,452 of service + panel + transformer sits in the cost. Three-phase service is a prerequisite, not an option.
